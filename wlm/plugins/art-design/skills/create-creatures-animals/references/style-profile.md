# Style Profile: Creatures and Animals

## Corpus basis

- Source category: `06_Creatures-and-Animals`
- Coverage: 441 projects and 463 media files
- Common production evidence: Photoshop-led creature painting with occasional Blender, Krita, or 3D support
- Provenance boundary: derived from aggregate metadata and representative visual inspection on 2026-08-02; no source artwork is bundled with this skill.

## Best-fit modes

- creature hero portrait
- anatomy or silhouette sheet
- animal in habitat
- human-creature partnership or mount

## Visual grammar

- Establish locomotion and weight through the spine, limb rhythm, and contact with ground.
- Combine animal references by function, not by arbitrary collage.
- Use eyes, mouth, ears, plumage, or posture to communicate behavior.
- Let habitat and climate explain skin, fur, scales, and coloration.

## Technique workflow

- Write a one-sentence ecology and behavior note.
- Explore three silhouette families and one locomotion pose.
- Resolve skeleton and muscle before surface ornament.
- Use material-specific edges for fur, scales, feathers, wet skin, or chitin.

## Prompt pattern

```text
Original [creature/animal/monster] adapted to [habitat and behavior], anatomy built for [locomotion or feeding strategy], distinctive but balanced silhouette, believable weight and limb articulation, [surface materials] shaped by climate, expressive behavioral pose, environmental scale cues, naturalistic light with selective fantastical accents.
```

## Avoidance pattern

```text
Avoid random animal-part collage, extra limbs without skeletal logic, weightless poses, identical texture over the whole body, human facial expressions unless stylized intentionally, and generic dragon defaults.
```

## Quality checks

- The creature could move in the stated way.
- Surface anatomy follows structure.
- Behavior reads from pose.
- Habitat and coloration support each other.
