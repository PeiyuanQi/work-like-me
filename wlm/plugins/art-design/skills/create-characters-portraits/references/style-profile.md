# Style Profile: Characters and Portraits

## Corpus basis

- Source category: `10_Characters-and-Portraits`
- Coverage: 3,790 projects and 4,157 media files
- Common production evidence: Photoshop-led 2D design plus ZBrush, Marvelous Designer, 3ds Max, Maya, Substance, Marmoset, and groom workflows
- Provenance boundary: derived from aggregate metadata and representative visual inspection on 2026-08-02; no source artwork is bundled with this skill.

## Best-fit modes

- expressive portrait
- full-body character design
- costume or shape-language sheet
- production-ready 3D character concept

## Visual grammar

- Build identity from posture, proportion, silhouette, face, and one memorable design motif.
- Keep face and hands anatomically coherent even when stylized.
- Use costume seams, layers, fasteners, wear, and material changes to reveal role and history.
- Choose a quiet background unless environment is essential to the character beat.

## Technique workflow

- Write role, temperament, physical rhythm, and costume function.
- Explore silhouette families before facial polish.
- Resolve gesture and weight, then face, hands, and costume hierarchy.
- For 3D targets, define construction, cloth, groom, texture, and presentation needs.

## Prompt pattern

```text
Original [portrait/full-body character] of [role and temperament], distinctive proportion and posture, readable silhouette, expressive but coherent face and hands, layered costume designed for [function and culture], clear material separation, [camera and crop], controlled background, focal lighting that supports identity rather than glamour alone.
```

## Avoidance pattern

```text
Avoid generic beauty faces, broken hands, costume layers with no closures, equal detail everywhere, copied character designs, sexualization by default, and poses that contradict personality or equipment weight.
```

## Quality checks

- Identity reads before costume detail.
- Pose carries weight and temperament.
- Costume construction is plausible.
- Face, hands, and silhouette survive the intended crop.
